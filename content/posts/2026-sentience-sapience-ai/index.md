Earlier this year, a friend shared with me a conversation he had with a frontier AI model about its experiences while it conversed with my friend — 
they were exploring various topics from life and awareness in living beings, but my friend was very carefully studying the expressions the AI chose to represent its output. 
Another friend shared with me a transcript of her father’s conversation with an AI where topics such as feelings, agency and control of one’s actions were discussed. 
Both these friends have been telling me how they converse with AI regularly, about everything — 
from research ideas, reading and finding new work literature, exploring new cities while traveling, finding new and happening restaurants, 
as well as considerably personal topics such as processing feelings, emotions and personal planning. 
I’ve read in the news about people regularly using AI as a friend, collaborator, personal therapist and even a romantic conversation partner!

I’ve always been hesitant to engage AI for uses other than writing simple programs and scripts or aggregating vast bodies of literature for quick summaries and sifting. 
Its difficult for me to relate to AI as beings of any sort, and my natural intraversion probably makes it harder. 
Perhaps more importantly, _I have not found a way to be able to think about the consequences of AI having any degree of sapience or sentience at all._
To show you what I mean by this conundrum, I illustrate several characterizations of what the AI might be _actually ``doing''_ when it converses with us through the apps.

But before we go there, lets lay some background of how other people, animals and pets converse with us in our daily lives without producing similar thoughts and conundrums in us.

# Parrots

Before AI came into our lives, humans have long been aware of several species who learned to understand humans and often and communicate with people.
The species that I have the most experience with are parrots. When I was a baby on all fours, I would walk to the parrot in my grandparents' home and be endlessly entertained.
As I grew older, I saw that the parrot **Tushki** was visibly overjoyed when I would approach him on a visit to my grandparents'. 
While I approached, Tushki would say in a sweet, ringing voice its own name with a lovely exclamation, the same way my grandma would call Tushki to express love.
In afternoons when it was time to have lunch, Tushki would tell me to go downstairs and have lunch -- sometimes after he heard my grandma calling for me, 
other times on its own _even before my grandma's call_, and I used to be surprised to go down and see that lunch was indeed ready!
There were several others ways in which Tushki could talk to the young child that I was, and my time with him was one of the most memorable parts of my early childhood.

I am sure you have had similar experiences with animals, or perhaps know someone who does. After all, the deep connection with birds and pets is a very common thing in our societies.i
But have you ever wondered how it could be that they understand us at all? What could be happening in _their eyes, their ears and their minds_ as they are talking to us?
After all, it must be readily available to our imaginations that from their lives, our society and we must appear largely alien and incomprehensible.
For Tushki was so sweet with greeting me and telling me meal is ready (so much so that I _still_ have fond memories of him) but did he know what it is like to have two hands with fingers?
Similarly, I'm sure most people will agree that he probablg couldn't comprehend a life with clothes, clocks, homes, and so on.

I will consider the example of parrots and other birds who can mimic human speech and expressions, 
are known to build significant vocabulary and even understand and reason with them to some extent.
